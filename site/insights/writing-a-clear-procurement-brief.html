How to write a clearer procurement brief

A focused specification helps suppliers respond with comparable options and fewer avoidable follow-ups.

A useful procurement brief describes the outcome first, then the specification. Start with what the item or service must help your organisation do.

For products, include quantities, acceptable technical requirements, delivery location and any standards or compatibility needs. For services, outline the site, scope, timing and how completion will be assessed.

Separate essential requirements from preferences. This makes it easier to compare quotations fairly and identify where a supplier needs clarification.

Before sending a request, confirm the approving contact and the process for receiving questions. A well-structured brief saves time for both the organisation and its suppliers.

This guide provides general information, not regulated professional, legal, accounting or cybersecurity advice. Requirements vary by organisation and context.
